0. FOREWORD 

0.1 This Indian Standard was adopted by the Indian Standards Institution 
on 5 October 1981, after the draft finalized by the Footwear Sectional Com- 
mittee, had been approved by the Chemical Division Council. 

0.2 A Series of Indian Standards on lasts is being compiled for grinding 
manufactures of lasts and footwear. The principles on which this standard, 
the fourth in the series, is based are given below. 

0.3 Two-third from the rear, including the leg height portion, is concerned 
with the least mobile part of the feet; as such this portion need not change 
with fashion or for a specific requirement It is mainly the fore-part, its 
shape and the length of the insole from ball to toe to the last that alter with 
fashion. The modern factory practice is to standardize the back portion 
(approximately 2/3 between heel and ball) for a particular heel height. 

0.4 From the designing point of view it is not desirable to lay down any 
rigorous specification either for the shape or distribution of material on 
last; only the dimensional aspect is followed, with guidance on material, 
mechanical requirements, sampling and test methods as well as various 
constructional requirements of the metal lasts for knee boots. 

0.5 In this standard, as far as feasible, rationalized values of English sizes, 
which are in vouge in India have been prescribed. Equivalent Paris points 
shall be in accordance with IS : 1638-1969*. It is hoped that the footwear 
industry which is presently following English sizes or Paris points or both 
will progressively switch over to an internationally acknowledged rational 
metric size system. The Sectional Committee further took note of the 
'Mondopoint' as a possible solution in the future for size and fitting 
standardization in the metric system, but decided not to prescribe this 
system in this specification in view of its being still in the developmental 
stage. 

0.6 For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this 
standard is complied with, the final value, observed or calculated, expressing 
the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance with 
IS : 2-1960t. The number of significant places retained in the rounded off 
value should be the same as that of the specified value in this standard.

5. PACKING 

5,1 Lasts shall be properly packed in boxes made of wooden planks {see 
IS: 1503-1967*). 

5.1.1 The case shall be lined with waterproof packing paper ( see 
IS : 1 398-1 968t) along bottom and sides. A layer of shavings or straw 
shall then be spread on the paper at the bottom of the case and a suitable 
number of lasts placed in rows. The space between individual lasts and 
between the lasts and the sides of the case shall be filled closely with paper 
cuttings, wood shavings or straw. On the lasts so arranged, a layer of wood 
shavings, straw or paper cuttings shall be spread. In this way layers of last 
shall be packed till the box is filled. The top layer shall also be covered 
with straw, wood shavings or paper cutting and the packing paper. 

5.1.2 Nails fixing the cover lid, shall be correctly driven in and shall 
not come out from the boards on the inner or outer surface of the box. 

5.1.3 The cases shall be secured against breakage in transit by binding 
with hoop iron or similar devices. 

5.1.4 In the case of card board boxes, they shall be secured by nylon 
strapping and corresponding metal seal and finally covered with gunny 
bags. 

6. SAMPLING AND CRITERIA FOR CONFORMITY 

6.1 For the purpose of ascertaining the conformity of the lasts for safety 
rubber knee boots to this specification, the scale of sampling and criteria 
for conformity shall be as given in Appendix B. 

7. TEST METHODS 

7.1 Visual Characteristics — . Examine the last visually for conformity to 
the approved specimen in respect of finish, appearance, surface defects, etc. 

7.2 Dimensional Characteristics 

7.2.1 Determine the length of the lasts by size-stick, based on the 
standard length as given in Table 1. 

7.2.2 To ensure that the longitudinal and transverse sQcXions as well as 
ball and heel parts and bottom shape of any size in a set of lasts agree with 
the specifications for shape and measurements, use graded insole patterns 
and various graded control profiles, including back curve of the heel leg, 
toe and bottom profile for the entire size range. 

Note — The patterns for insole and profiles shall be cut to the master or model size, 
from which the remaining sizes shall be graded. It gives length, shape, width of the 
fore-part seat and leg and to this extent it is suitable for testing the lasts. Grades arc 
fixed arbitrarily and adopted by custom in the last industry.

7.2.5.1 Approximate position for location of the ball and instep girth 
measurement in insole pattern is shown in Fig. 4. This system requires 
little skill and may be performed very easily without the help of any 
apparatus and as such may be considered as standard practice under the

Note — Although the joint girth measurement is taken diagonally in a transverse 
direction, but makers of lasts take this measurement at arbitrary points; this enables 
them to place the measuring tape flat to the wood of the last. Generally this takes 
in either the inside or outside joint, but never both. 

7.2.6 Following the system described above, check the correctness of 
position of pin-points in this manner; the bottom of the last, the insole, 
checked by marked lines is superimposed, so that starting from the toe part, 
it coincides all over with the bottom of the last. 

7.2.6.1 Pin-points at the joints ( inside and outside) shall be exactly 
located on the last at the ends of lines above the counter ( feather edge ) of 
the lasts at a distance of 10 mm. 

7.2.6.2 Correctness of the position of the pin-points located on the 
body of the last at the base of the instep ( along with centre line of the last ) 
is checked either with the measuring tape with the help of two pin-points of 
the joints for measurement of the ball girth; or by the longitudinal front 
profile which with the mark point is put on the last and the coincidence of 
the pin-point on the last with the point of the profile is observed. 

7.2.7 For the determination of the correctness of the position of the 
pin-point for measurement of instep girth on the cone of the last, the 
following method is recommended. 

7.2.7.1 This position is represented on the last, along with the middle 
of the cone, at a distance of 60 mm above the ball joint pin-point which is 
located at the base of the instep ( cone ) ( see Fig. 5 ). 

7.2.7.2 To ascertain the girth of the instep, the tape shall lie flat and 
close to the last on the selected points. 

Note — Like the joint measurement the instep measurement may not also be located 
exactly by rule. Half standard length of the last (excluding the difference between the 
length of the insole and the foot), on an imaginary line through the length may be 
given as the approximate position. The largest girth at that point probably answer 
the purpose best. 

7.2.8 To ascertain the girth of the ankle, the tape shall lie flat around the 
heel seat and ankle, as shown in Fig. 4 and 5. 

7.2.9 To ascertain the girth of the leg at 10, 20, 30 and 40 cm, the tape 
shall lie flat as shown in Fig. 5 and parallel to the top line. 

Note — Leg height shall be determined by placing the last on a flat surface 
(without putting cny heel) and measuring vertically from the heel seat point at the 
required height.

Note — The actual or stick-length of last may vary from the standard length of the 
insole pattern. The insole length shall be always 2 and 3 mm shorter than the stick-

length of a last is the distance from the centre of the curve at the back to the extreme tip

7.2.12 Measurement of Heel Height — Place the last on a level surface. 
Raise the seat of the last until the tread line touches the level surface. Then 
measure the heel height from the seat position to the level surface. Take 
care that the seat of the last rests almost parallel to the ground.

parallel to the ground is the required toe spring.

B-2. NUMBER OF TESTS AND CRITERIA FOR CONFORMITY 
B-2.1 Number of Tests

satisfied the requirements for these characteristics, otherwise not.
